How to use FrameCapt

A practical walkthrough of the current build. Everything you capture stays on your computer.

Getting started

  1. Download the installer for your system and install it.
  2. Start FrameCapt. There is no account and nothing to sign in to. The Home tab shows a welcome card with your shortcuts; Got it hides it for good.
  3. Press Ctrl+Shift+3 anywhere, drag over part of the screen and press Enter. The screenshot opens in the editor and is already on your clipboard.

Closing the window keeps FrameCapt running in the tray so your shortcuts keep working. Quit from the tray menu, or turn Close to tray off in Settings.

The window

  • Top bar: the menus File, View and Help, and the command center in the middle. Press Ctrl+K to search commands and captures, or Ctrl+Shift+P for commands only.
  • Icon rail on the left: Home, Library, Guides and Settings, with Keyboard shortcuts (?) at the bottom.
  • Tabs: Home is always first. Each capture, opened image, recording or guide opens in its own tab and keeps its own undo history. Switch with Ctrl+Tab or Alt+1…9, close with Ctrl+W. A dot on a tab means unsaved changes.
  • Home has tiles for every capture type, the recording options and your latest captures. Hover a tile to see its shortcut.

Screenshots

Type Shortcut What happens
Screen Ctrl+Shift+1 One monitor: captured at once. Several: click the one you want (or press Enter).
Window Ctrl+Shift+2 Pick from a list of open windows (type to search). Minimized windows are not listed.
Region Ctrl+Shift+3 The screen freezes; drag a rectangle, move or resize it, then Enter. Esc or right-click cancels.
All screens Ctrl+Shift+4 Every monitor in one image, laid out like your desktop.

Region selection with the keyboard

Arrow keys start a centered selection and move it. Hold Shift to move 10 px at a time, Alt to resize. A region stays on one monitor.

Copy and save

  • Every screenshot is copied to the clipboard right away (you can turn this off in Settings > Screenshots).
  • Ctrl+S saves as PNG or JPEG and lets you pick a name.
  • Ctrl+Shift+S (Quick save) skips the dialog and saves to your screenshots folder, with Show in folder and Undo.
  • Ctrl+C copies the edited image again.
  • Ctrl+O opens any image from disk in a new editor tab.

The editor

Screenshots open in the editor. The tools are crop, arrow, rectangle, text and redact, with undo (Ctrl+Z) and redo (Ctrl+Shift+Z).

Redaction

Redaction draws a solid, opaque box. It is flattened into the saved file, the clipboard image and the Library thumbnail, so what is under it cannot be recovered from those. Note that FrameCapt keeps an editable original (with the pixels underneath) in its own data folder so you can change annotations later. If you do not want that, turn Keep editable originals off in Settings > Screenshots, or use Delete editable data on the item.

Editing a saved screenshot

In the Library, choose Edit. With an editable original every annotation can still be moved, restyled or deleted. Save replaces the image; Save as copy keeps both.

Recording

Start from Home > Record or a shortcut: Screen (Ctrl+Shift+5), Window (Ctrl+Shift+6) or Region (Ctrl+Shift+7). Home also offers All screens and Multiple… to record several screens or windows together.

Options (Home or Settings > Recording)

  • Quality: 1080p, or Source for the full screen resolution.
  • Frame rate: 30 (default) or 60.
  • Microphone and System audio are separate switches and are mixed into one track. System audio is Windows only.
  • Countdown: 3-2-1 before recording starts (on by default).
  • Follow mouse: 1.5×, 2× or 3× zoom that glides to wherever the pointer is (screen recordings).
  • Camera: add your webcam to the recording.

While recording

A small floating toolbar shows the timer with Pause/Resume, Mute, a camera button to take a screenshot, and Stop. The toolbar and countdown are kept out of the video on Windows.

  • Pause or resume: Ctrl+Shift+9. Paused time is not recorded.
  • Stop: Ctrl+Shift+0, the toolbar, the tray, or the same record shortcut again.
  • Screenshots taken during a recording go straight to your screenshots folder; no editor pops up into the video.

Video is written to disk as you record, so long recordings do not fill memory. Recording refuses to start below 1 GB of free disk space and stops safely below 500 MB.

If something crashes

The next start shows “We found an unfinished recording” with Recover and Discard. Recovery is best effort: the last second or so may be missing.

Formats and MP4 export

Recordings are saved as WebM by default. In Settings > Recording, Save recordings as converts each finished recording to MP4, MKV or GIF, and Compression (Light, Balanced, Strong) makes files smaller.

  • Export MP4 on any recording in the Library makes an H.264 + AAC copy, with progress and Cancel.
  • Save in another format… makes a converted copy, optionally smaller (1920, 1280, 960 or 640 px wide).
  • GIF is for clips up to 60 seconds and has no sound.
  • A conversion only replaces the original after the new file checks out; the original goes to the Recycle Bin.

Video editor

In the Library choose Edit video. The editor never changes your recording; Export writes a new file named <recording> (edited).mp4 next to it.

  • Trim: drag the ends of the clip on the timeline.
  • Cut a piece out: drag over a range (or press I and O), then Delete.
  • Hide something: Redact, Blur, Pixelate or Spotlight boxes, each with its own start and length.
  • Crop to Free, 16:9, 9:16, 1:1 or 4:3.
  • Text, images and logos with fonts, colours, outlines and fades.
  • Audio: add music or a voice-over track, set volumes and fades.
  • Export to MP4, WebM or GIF at the original size or smaller.

Space plays and pauses, arrow keys step a frame (Shift: a second), Ctrl+mouse wheel zooms the timeline. Your edits are saved automatically and come back next time you open the recording.

Step guides

Like the old Windows Steps Recorder. Press Ctrl+Shift+8 (or the Steps tile) and do your task. Each time you rest the mouse on something for a moment, FrameCapt takes a step screenshot and marks where the pointer was.

  • The pill at the bottom of the screen has Auto, Capture step, Pause, Done and Cancel.
  • Done saves a folder of numbered PNGs and opens the guide: edit the title, write captions, reorder or delete steps, or open a step in the editor.
  • Export as numbered PNGs, one self-contained HTML page, an MP4 slideshow or a GIF.

Library and folders

The Library lists your screenshots, recordings and guides. Search by name, date or type, sort, and switch between grid and list.

  • Ctrl-click or Shift-click to select several, then save copies or remove them in one go.
  • Right-click a card for Edit, Open, Show in folder, Copy, Move to…, Delete file and more.
  • Drag a card out of the window to drop the actual file into chat, email or Explorer.
  • Remove from history only removes the entry. Delete file moves the file to the Recycle Bin.
  • Find existing captures (the ⋯ menu) brings back captures after a reinstall.

Folders

Folders in the sidebar are real folders on your disk, created in both your screenshots and recordings folders. Drag captures onto a folder to move them, and right-click a folder and choose Set as save location to send new captures there.

Settings and tray

  • General: theme (follow system, light, dark), launch at login, close to tray, notifications.
  • Screenshots: format, JPEG quality, what happens after a capture, clipboard.
  • Recording: quality, frame rate, audio, countdown, save format, compression.
  • Shortcuts: change or turn off any shortcut. A clash offers Swap; Reset all shortcuts restores the defaults.
  • Storage: where screenshots and recordings are saved.

The search box at the top of Settings filters every option. The tray icon (red while recording) has the capture actions and Quit.

Where files go

What Windows Linux
Screenshots Pictures\FrameCapt ~/Pictures/FrameCapt
Recordings and exports Videos\FrameCapt ~/Videos/FrameCapt
Settings, history, logs %APPDATA%\FrameCapt ~/.config/FrameCapt

File names look like FrameCapt 2026-10-02 at 14.05.09.png. Existing files are never overwritten. Change the folders in Settings > Storage.

Linux notes

The Linux build is experimental. What to expect:

  • Runs on X11, or through XWayland on a Wayland session. Native Wayland windows may be missing or black.
  • No system audio; the microphone works.
  • The recording toolbar may appear in full-screen recordings.
  • Global shortcuts, tray and microphone have not been verified on a real desktop yet. Reports are welcome.
  • Shortcuts your desktop already uses (for example Ctrl+Alt+T) are refused in Settings.

Troubleshooting

SmartScreen warns about the installer

The installer is not code-signed yet. Check its SHA-256 against SHA256SUMS.txt on the release page, then choose More info > Run anyway.

A shortcut does nothing

Another app owns that key combination. Settings > Shortcuts shows “used by another app”; pick a different one. Ctrl+Shift+0 can clash with the Windows input-language hotkey.

Part of the picture is black

Windows blocks capturing DRM-protected video (streaming sites, some players). That area is black in screenshots and recordings.

A window is missing from the list

Minimized windows cannot be captured. Restore the window and try again.

No system audio or a silent microphone

Check your default output device and Windows microphone privacy settings, and the device chosen in FrameCapt. The toolbar shows a badge if a source drops out mid-recording.

Reporting a problem

The log is at %APPDATA%\FrameCapt\logs\main.log (Linux: ~/.config/FrameCapt/logs/main.log). It contains diagnostic text only, but read it before attaching it to an issue.
